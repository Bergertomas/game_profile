# Game Profile — Calibration Round 2 Report v0.1
**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Rubric tested:** Game Profile Scoring Rubric v0.2  
**Purpose:** Calibrate the low/mid range, test “strong idea / weak execution” cases, and decide whether the rubric is ready to freeze for engineering.

---

# 1. Corpus

1. Banishers: Ghosts of New Eden
2. Vampyr
3. Observer: System Redux
4. Forspoken
5. Redfall — final/current Update 4 state
6. Crimson Desert — current Aug 2026 state

The first three were inherited from the Tomas Fit source as known examples where premise/story interest and actual play quality could diverge. Forspoken and Redfall were selected as lower-range open-world/execution anchors. Crimson Desert was added as a current ambitious sandbox whose strengths and weaknesses cut across multiple dimensions.

---

# 2. Result

**Round 2 passes.**

The rubric now produces credible profiles through the mid and low range without requiring a new dimension or a return to caps/bonuses.

Most importantly:

- Banishers can score very highly for Story/Theme while remaining only middling in Agency/Pacing/Structure.
- Vampyr can be rough in Execution/Pacing while scoring extremely highly in Medium-Specific Craft because its feeding/XP/citizen-health systems create genuine moral agency.
- Observer can be low-agency and still be a compelling interactive work through atmosphere, psychological investigation and sensory recontextualization.
- Forspoken can expose genuinely strong movement/agency while scoring weakly in story, structure and pacing.
- Redfall establishes a real lower-range anchor where multiple systems are merely weak or incoherent rather than “not personally preferred.”
- Crimson Desert demonstrates that scale, combat freedom and sandbox possibility can be major strengths even when narrative cohesion and usability are much less accomplished.

No structural change to the eight dimensions is required.

# 4. Banishers: Ghosts of New Eden

## Profile shape
Narrative/theme-forward action RPG whose story and moral decisions are substantially stronger than its repeated combat and side-content structure.

### Primary pull
A sincere, unusually intimate love story built around consequential judgments about death, guilt and sacrifice.

### Primary risk
The action-RPG scaffolding repeatedly interrupts stronger narrative material with combat and side activities that do not develop enough across the runtime.

### Great fit if…
- Character relationships and morally uncomfortable choices drive your RPG interest.
- You enjoy supernatural investigation and historical-gothic atmosphere.
- You can accept merely competent combat in exchange for stronger narrative payoff.

### Know before buying…
- Combat is functional rather than exceptional.
- Enemy variety and progression flatten later.
- Optional activities can feel like padding.

### Probably not for you if…
- An action RPG lives or dies for you on combat depth.
- Repeated fights between story beats feel like chores.
- You want a tightly focused campaign.

## Calibration lesson
This is the exact case the original Tomas model needed “Execution Trust” caps for. The public model does not. A profile with Story 9 / Theme 9 beside Pacing 6 / Structure 6.5 communicates the conflict directly.

# 5. Vampyr

## Profile shape
Strong gothic narrative/systemic morality with rough combat, cumbersome traversal and slow dialogue-heavy pacing.

### Primary pull
Its citizen system turns the central vampire fantasy into a real mechanical temptation: killing people makes the player stronger while damaging the social ecosystem they have learned to care about.

### Primary risk
Imprecise combat, repeated street encounters, lack of fast travel and long conversational stretches create significant friction.

### Great fit if…
- You want vampire morality expressed through systems rather than only cutscenes.
- Investigating interconnected NPCs is more interesting than clearing combat arenas.
- Gothic London atmosphere is a major draw.

### Know before buying…
- Dialogue/investigation dominates much of the runtime.
- Combat is serviceable but imprecise.
- Traversal becomes increasingly repetitive.

### Probably not for you if…
- You need crisp action combat.
- Long conversations and repeated routes drain your momentum.
- You dislike games that allow irreversible social consequences.

## Calibration lesson
Vampyr is a critical proof for **Medium-Specific Craft**. Rough execution does not erase the ingenuity of linking XP, murder, NPC knowledge and district health. The model can now say both things at once.

# 6. Observer: System Redux

## Profile shape
Low-direct-agency cyberpunk horror whose atmosphere, sensory design and interactive psychological investigation carry the experience.

### Primary pull
A suffocating cyberpunk setting and brain-hacking sequences that use audiovisual disorientation and first-person exploration as part of the horror.

### Primary risk
Moment-to-moment interaction is limited, while forced stealth and repetitive exploration can feel more obstructive than meaningful.

### Great fit if…
- Atmosphere and psychological horror matter more than combat.
- You enjoy first-person investigation and environmental storytelling.
- Experimental sensory sequences appeal.

### Know before buying…
- It is closer to an exploratory horror/adventure game than an action game.
- Stealth sections are divisive.
- System Redux fixes many original technical shortcomings, but limited gameplay remains deliberate.

### Probably not for you if…
- You require mechanical depth or combat agency.
- Walking-sim/adventure structures feel passive.
- Heavy visual distortion is unpleasant.

## Calibration lesson
Like Alien: Isolation in Round 1, Observer confirms that **low Agency is descriptive, not punitive**. It also validates scoring the current System Redux product rather than freezing the original 2017 technical state.

# 7. Forspoken

## Profile shape
Strong magical traversal and decent expressive combat inside a bland, repetitive open-world structure and weak narrative wrapper.

### Primary pull
Fast magical parkour creates a genuinely distinct way of crossing a large 3D space.

### Primary risk
Generic activities, weak narrative/characterization and stop-start pacing repeatedly get in the way of the game’s best movement systems.

### Great fit if…
- Traversal itself is a major pleasure.
- Large elemental toolkits appeal.
- You are happy ignoring substantial optional content.

### Know before buying…
- Combat improves as the spell kit opens.
- The open world relies heavily on repeated activity structures.
- Story delivery frequently interrupts movement.

### Probably not for you if…
- Strong writing/characters are required.
- Repeated open-world icons rapidly feel meaningless.
- You dislike having the game halt player movement for exposition.

## Calibration lesson
Forspoken creates our cleanest **5–8 split**: mediocre Story/Structure/Pacing with genuinely strong Agency. That is exactly the kind of distinction an aggregate 6–7/10 obscures.

# 8. Redfall — final Update 4 state

## Scope note
This profile scores the final/current playable version after Game Update 4, not the 2023 launch build.

Confidence remains Medium because most full critical reviews evaluated the earlier state; the final update added offline play, pause, Community Standing and revamped Nest/Neighborhood systems, but there is limited equivalent full-review coverage of the final build.

## Profile shape
A low-range anchor: multiple core systems are individually underdeveloped and fail to combine into the systemic Arkane-style experience implied by the premise.

### Primary pull
A visually distinctive vampire-town premise with some enjoyable weapons/abilities, particularly in cooperative play.

### Primary risk
The world, shooting, AI, mission structure and systemic interactions rarely generate enough depth or surprise to sustain the premise.

### Great fit if…
- You specifically want a lightweight vampire co-op shooter.
- You are comfortable with repetitive mission structures.
- You value atmosphere/theme more than systemic depth.

### Know before buying…
- The final update meaningfully improved usability and preservation.
- Offline solo and pausing now exist.
- Those changes do not fundamentally redesign the core combat/world.

### Probably not for you if…
- You expect Dishonored/Prey-style systemic creativity.
- Empty-feeling open worlds are a major aversion.
- Enemy AI and encounter variety need to carry an FPS for you.

## Calibration lesson
Redfall establishes that 4–5 scores are usable without hyperbole. A 4.5 Structure score means systems/content are weakly organized and repetitive; it does not require declaring the product literally nonfunctional.

# 9. Crimson Desert — current Aug 2026 state

## Scope note
Released March 19, 2026. The game remains actively updated; an August economy overhaul added 133 trading posts and a supply/demand system. Profile should therefore retain a current-state cutoff.

## Profile shape
Massive sandbox ambition, high combat/traversal freedom and spectacular world presentation with much weaker narrative coherence, usability and focus.

### Primary pull
A huge fantasy sandbox containing an extraordinary range of combat, traversal, creatures, vehicles and systemic diversions.

### Primary risk
The “more is more” design philosophy can produce clumsy controls, uneven usability and a diffuse narrative/structural identity.

### Great fit if…
- Discovery and sandbox possibility matter more than tight narrative focus.
- You enjoy learning a large combat toolkit.
- Huge feature-rich worlds excite rather than overwhelm you.

### Know before buying…
- Story is substantially weaker than the sandbox.
- Some boss/control/UI friction remains.
- The product continues to change through substantial patches.

### Probably not for you if…
- You prefer authored focus over feature density.
- Busy controls/UI quickly become exhausting.
- You require a strongly coherent protagonist/story arc.

## Calibration lesson
Crimson Desert proves that **Content Density is not the same thing as Focus**. A game can contain enormous amounts of meaningful activity while still scoring only moderately for Structure/Pacing because the experience lacks editorial restraint.

# 10. Low/mid-range calibration anchors

Round 2 now gives us usable working meanings for public dimension scores.

## 9.0–10.0 — Exceptional
A defining strength of the product and often a genre/reference-point quality.

## 8.0–8.5 — Strong
Clearly above ordinary execution; a meaningful reason to choose the game.

## 7.0–7.5 — Good / solid
Works well and supports the experience, with visible limitations.

## 6.0–6.5 — Mixed / compromised
Functional and sometimes effective, but recurring limitations materially affect the experience.

## 5.0–5.5 — Weak
The dimension offers some value, but shortcomings are persistent and difficult to ignore.

## 3.5–4.5 — Poor / limited
The dimension contributes little or is repeatedly undermined by its implementation.

## 0–3.0 — Absent / severely deficient
Reserved for genuinely absent offerings or extremely broken execution; still requires context because absence can be intentional (for example, conventional story in a pure sandbox).

### Important
These labels are **editorial calibration language**, not UI grades.
The public visualization should emphasize profile shape, not “red = failure.”

# 11. Final rubric decision

No ninth dimension is needed.
No aggregate score is needed.
No cap/bonus system is needed.

**Rubric v0.2 is ready to freeze as Game Profile Rubric v1.0**, with two minor additions:

1. Add these low/mid-range calibration anchors.
2. Add a rule that active/live post-launch games may remain Provisional when substantial systems continue changing.

# 12. Radar / spider chart decision

**Approved as a core secondary visualization.**

The radar chart is unusually appropriate because the product’s thesis is literally the **shape of a game**.

## Design contract

- Exactly eight axes.
- Axis order is fixed across every page.
- Default single-game radar near the score summary.
- Exact numeric score rows remain directly below/alongside it.
- The chart is never the only accessible representation.
- No “total area” score.
- No green/red good/bad coloring.
- Comparison overlay maximum: **two games**.
- Three/four-game compare uses aligned bars/table instead; multiple polygons become unreadable.
- Tooltips/tap reveal exact score and dimension definition.
- Mobile version must remain legible; allow the labels to move outside the polygon or use abbreviated labels with a legend.
- Unknown dimensions must not be rendered as zero. Use a gap/dashed uncertainty treatment.
- Pre-release ranges should render as uncertainty bands/secondary polygons only if the implementation remains visually clean.

## Recommended radar axis order

Use a conceptual clockwise grouping rather than the rubric's database order:

1. Story & Characters
2. Thematic & Emotional
3. Atmosphere & World
4. Medium-Specific Craft
5. Agency & Satisfaction
6. Execution & Polish
7. Structure & Focus
8. Pacing & Time Respect

This creates an intuitive transition from **meaning/world → interactivity/play → delivery/time** and makes silhouettes easier to mentally compare.

The underlying canonical dimension IDs remain unchanged.
