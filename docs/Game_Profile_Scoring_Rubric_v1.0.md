# Game Profile — Scoring Rubric v1.0
**Derived from Tomas Game Fit Score v1.2, generalized for public use**  
**Status:** Calibrated and engineering-ready after Rounds 1–2  
**Date:** 2026-08-06

---

# 1. Purpose

This rubric describes a game. It does not attempt to predict whether every person will like it.

## Foundational interpretation

A dimension score measures the **strength and extent of that experiential offering**, not universal goodness.

A low score can be valuable neutral information. For example:
- a survival sandbox may have little conventional Story & Character Investment,
- a deliberately helpless horror game may have limited direct Agency,
- a mechanically focused game may have modest Thematic Impact.

Those facts do not automatically make the game bad. Because of this:
- the public product does not calculate an overall average score,
- low scores must not automatically use red/error visual semantics,
- user fit is interpreted later through the profile rather than baked into the score.

## Required evaluation scope

Every evaluation must declare:
- product / edition,
- campaign or mode scope,
- platform scope,
- current patch/build or current-state cutoff where relevant,
- evidence cutoff date.

If different modes materially change the experience, create separate evaluations rather than averaging them together.

If platform technical quality materially differs, Technical Stability may use platform-specific overrides/notes.

Each game receives eight dimension profiles. Each dimension is built from five subcriteria scored:

- 0 = notably weak / absent where expected
- 0.5 = weak
- 1 = competent / mixed / ordinary
- 1.5 = strong
- 2 = exceptional

The dimension total is 0–10.

`Unknown` is allowed when evidence is insufficient.

Do not infer quality from popularity.
Do not infer quality from budget.
Do not penalize a genre for being that genre.
Do not use a personal preference as a quality judgment.

# 2. Dimension 1 — Story & Character Investment

## Core question
**How effectively does the game create and sustain narrative/character investment on its own terms?**

This is broader than “literary writing.” A game may create narrative investment through character, mystery, continuity, lore, dramatic stakes, environmental storytelling or franchise context.

### 1. Story Hook & Stakes
Does the central situation create a reason to care or continue?

### 2. Character Investment
Are central characters legible, compelling, affecting or interesting enough to carry the experience?

### 3. Narrative Coherence
Do plot, motivations and world rules hold together at the level the game requires?

### 4. Narrative Momentum & Payoff
Does the story develop rather than merely exist? Are setup and payoff proportionate?

### 5. World/Lore Integration
Does worldbuilding, lore, continuity or environmental narrative meaningfully strengthen investment?

## Boundaries
This dimension is **not**:
- “does the reviewer like this genre?”
- atmosphere by itself,
- philosophical depth by itself,
- production value.

---

# 3. Dimension 2 — Execution & Polish

## Core question
**How reliably and competently does the finished product deliver what it is trying to do?**

The source Tomas model used “Execution Trust.” For a public released-game profile, we score delivered execution rather than personal trust.

### 1. Dramatic/Writing Execution
Dialogue, scene construction, delivery and dramatic competence relative to the game’s intent.

### 2. Gameplay Execution
Responsiveness, clarity, consistency, animation/feedback and implementation quality.

### 3. Technical Stability
Performance, bugs, crashes, streaming/stutter and major technical problems, with platform context where necessary.

### 4. Production Cohesion
Do art, audio, UX, content and systems feel finished and coherent rather than visibly compromised?

### 5. Consistency
Does quality hold across the runtime, or do major sections/systems collapse?

## Platform rule
If platform performance differs materially, store platform-specific Technical Stability overrides/notes. Do not hide severe PC/console differences inside a single unexplained number.

When platform variance is large:
- show a public platform-performance warning,
- lower confidence in a platform-agnostic Execution score,
- allow the selected platform to alter the Technical Stability subcriterion.

---

# 4. Dimension 3 — Structure & Focus

## Core question
**How well is the game’s form organized around its intended experience?**

This replaces personal “Form Compatibility.”

A Soulslike is not penalized for being a Soulslike. An open world is not penalized for being large. We assess whether structure is intentional, usable and appropriately focused.

### 1. Structural Intentionality
Do linear/open/hub/mission/run structures support the game’s goals?

### 2. Navigation & Information Legibility
Does the game provide enough environmental, interface or systemic information for its intended navigation model to be learnable and coherent?

**Intent rule:** absence of a map, compass, quest marker or explicit objective tracker is not inherently a penalty. Judge whether the game's chosen information model communicates what a reasonably attentive player needs.

### 3. Repetition Control
Does the structure reuse content intelligently, or lean on filler/repetition beyond its value?

### 4. UX / Interaction Friction
Menus, inventory, traversal, checkpointing, onboarding and interface friction.

### 5. Content Focus
How much of the game feels purposeful versus padded, duplicated or obligatory?

## Important rule
“Large” is not bad.
“Small” is not automatically focused.
Judge signal-to-noise.

---

# 5. Dimension 4 — Agency & Satisfaction

## Core question
**Does the act of playing create meaningful, satisfying action/decision/reward loops?**

This is one of the most important inheritances from Tomas Fit.

### 1. Moment-to-Moment Agency
Does player input meaningfully influence the immediate situation?

### 2. Toolset / Choice Depth
Are there interesting tactical, strategic, expressive or systemic options appropriate to the genre?

### 3. Reward Rhythm
Does action produce satisfying feedback, progress, discovery, mastery or narrative reward?

### 4. Failure / Friction Fairness
When the game resists the player, is the resistance readable and proportionate to its design?

### 5. Capability Balance
Does the player have enough capability to engage with the pressure the game creates?

## Horror interpretation
High agency does not require power fantasy.
A horror game may score highly when fear is paired with meaningful resource, movement, combat, avoidance or decision agency.

A deliberately helpless game can still be excellent overall, but its **agency score should honestly reflect its design** rather than being “rescued” because the helplessness is intentional.

---

# 6. Dimension 5 — Pacing & Time Respect

## Core question
**How well does the game earn the time it asks from the player?**

This dimension does not reward shortness. It rewards justified time.

### 1. Opening Effectiveness
How effectively does the game establish its loop, stakes or appeal?

### 2. Momentum Maintenance
Does the experience continue developing or repeatedly stall?

### 3. Runtime Justification
Does the amount of content justify the runtime?

### 4. Session / Progress Rhythm
Does the game create appropriate and legible units of progress for its design, whether those units are short missions, long survival days, runs, quests, chapters or gradual simulation growth?

Do not reward short-session convenience by default.

### 5. Content Density
How much meaningful gameplay/story/discovery exists relative to downtime, chores or filler?

## Core principle
**Long is fine. Empty is not. Slow is fine when rich.**

---

# 7. Dimension 6 — Atmosphere & World Pull

## Core question
**How strongly does the game create a place, mood and sensory identity?**

This dimension transfers almost directly from Tomas Fit.

### 1. Sense of Place
Do locations feel distinct, grounded and inhabitable?

### 2. Mood Strength
Does the game sustain an intentional emotional/sensory tone?

### 3. Audiovisual Identity
Art direction, sound, music and audiovisual coherence.

### 4. World Coherence / Myth
Does the world feel like it has rules, history, culture or meaningful internal continuity?

### 5. Memory Residue
Does the world/mood leave distinctive places, sounds, images or sensations in memory?

## Boundary
Graphics fidelity alone does not equal atmosphere.

---

# 8. Dimension 7 — Thematic & Emotional Impact

## Core question
**How effectively does the game create emotional or thematic meaning beyond functional plot progression?**

This replaces Tomas-specific “Personal / Thematic Resonance.”

We are not scoring whether the themes match the evaluator’s life.

### 1. Thematic Clarity
Does the game meaningfully engage with themes, whether explicitly or implicitly?

### 2. Emotional Power
Do characters/events/systems create genuine emotional response appropriate to the intent?

### 3. Theme–Character Integration
Are themes embodied through characters and situations rather than only stated?

### 4. Philosophical / Mythic Weight
Where the game reaches for larger ideas, does it give them substance?

### 5. Lasting Impact
Does the emotional/thematic material continue to resonate after play?

## Calibration rule inherited from Tomas Fit
“On-paper themes” do not automatically score highly.
A game containing grief, fatherhood, identity, sacrifice, etc. must actually land those themes through execution.

---

# 9. Dimension 8 — Medium-Specific Craft

## Core question
**How meaningfully does the game use interactivity itself rather than merely delivering content through a game-shaped container?**

The Tomas source model treated this as four base criteria plus a meaningful-agency boost. For the public product, normalize it to five equal subcriteria.

### 1. Mechanics–Meaning Integration
Do mechanics reinforce narrative, emotion, theme or world?

### 2. Player Recontextualization
Does the game meaningfully change the player’s understanding of prior actions/assumptions?

### 3. Interactive Revelation / Discovery
Does learning through doing/exploring create value that passive media would lose?

### 4. Medium Irreplaceability
Would the core experience lose something essential if converted directly into film/TV/prose?

### 5. Meaningful Agency
Do player choices/actions create consequences, complicity, expression or meaning?

## Important rule
Most excellent games will not score 10 here.
This is not a general “gameplay quality” category. That is partly captured by Agency and Execution.

# 10. Experience-tag framework

Tags communicate **what the player must like/tolerate**, without turning those traits into universal penalties.

## Structure
- linear
- hub-based
- open-world
- mission-based
- run-based
- sandbox
- systemic

## Narrative
- story-heavy
- dialogue-heavy
- lore-heavy
- environmental-storytelling
- choice-consequence
- cutscene-heavy

## Play emphasis
- combat-heavy
- stealth-heavy
- exploration-heavy
- puzzle-heavy
- traversal-heavy
- buildcraft-heavy
- management-heavy

## Friction / commitment
- backtracking
- grind
- repetition
- run-reset
- high-punishment
- difficult-checkpointing
- inventory-pressure
- resource-pressure
- reading-dense
- complex-onboarding

## Mood / pressure
- horror
- sustained-tension
- helplessness-sections
- power-fantasy
- melancholy
- comedic
- cozy

## Social
- co-op-forward
- couch-friendly
- multiplayer-dependent
- PvP-forward

## Technical / temporal
- dated-friction
- technical-instability
- performance-sensitive

Intensity values where useful:
- low
- medium
- high

# 11. Public interpretation blocks

The profile must translate numbers into a buying decision.

## Great fit if…
Use concrete preference statements.

## Know before buying…
Use traits that may be a positive for some and friction for others.

## Probably not for you if…
Use strong mismatch statements.

### Style rules

Prefer:
- “You want…”
- “You enjoy…”
- “You’re comfortable with…”
- “You dislike…”

Avoid:
- “Casual gamers…”
- “Hardcore gamers…”
- “Real fans…”
- “Everyone…”
- “Objectively…”

---

# 12. Primary pull / primary risk

Every profile has exactly one of each.

## Primary pull
The single strongest reason this game earns attention.

Examples:
- “Exceptional survival-horror atmosphere paired with strong combat agency.”
- “Character-driven choice/consequence storytelling.”
- “Near-frictionless traversal and heroic action flow.”

## Primary risk
The single most likely source of mismatch or disappointment.

Examples:
- “Run-reset repetition is central to progression.”
- “The first several hours are deliberately slow.”
- “Combat exists mainly to connect narrative sections and lacks depth.”

These are not necessarily the highest/lowest numerical dimension.
They are purchase-decision summaries.

---

# 13. Evidence status

## VERIFIED
Substantial post-release evidence and profile is reasonably stable.

## PROVISIONAL
Released, but incomplete/conflicting evidence or rapid change.

## PRE-RELEASE
Not yet fully available; preview/demo/first-party evidence only.

Confidence:
- Low
- Medium
- High

A status is not a score.

---

# 14. Pre-release methodology

Pre-release scoring should be conservative.

### Safe to score early
Often:
- visible structure
- camera/form
- combat fundamentals from substantial hands-on
- audiovisual identity
- known multiplayer design

### Dangerous to score early
Often:
- narrative payoff
- runtime justification
- long-term repetition
- late-game systems
- technical stability at launch
- lasting impact

Use:
- unknown,
- range,
- low confidence

rather than guessing.

---

# 15. Calibration protocol

Before public launch, score at least 10 contrasting games.

For each:
1. Gather evidence.
2. Score all subcriteria.
3. Write rationale.
4. Compare score shape to lived/review consensus.
5. Identify whether any dimension is doing two jobs.
6. Adjust wording, not results, unless the rubric is genuinely broken.

Do not repeatedly rewrite the rubric simply because one game produces an uncomfortable but defensible profile.

---

# 16. Anti-bias checklist

Before publishing, ask:

- Did we score popularity instead of quality?
- Did a prestige studio receive unearned benefit?
- Did an indie receive an unearned “charming” benefit?
- Did genre preference leak into structure or agency?
- Did high production inflate atmosphere automatically?
- Did “important themes” inflate emotional impact without payoff?
- Did difficulty become a penalty rather than a described trait?
- Did length become a penalty rather than time justification?
- Did review consensus replace rubric judgment?
- Did a marketing claim become evidence of execution?

---

# 17. Current-state, mode and remake rules

## Current-state scoring
Score the current patched product at the declared evidence/build cutoff. Do not silently preserve launch-state technical problems after they are fixed, and do not silently overwrite historical evaluations after a material change.

## Mode-specific scoring
When game modes produce materially different profiles, create separate evaluations.

Example:
- The Long Dark — Survival mode
- The Long Dark — Wintermute / Story mode

## Remakes / remasters / re-releases
The eight dimensions score the **current playable product**.

Store separately:
- source/original game,
- release type (remake/remaster/re-release),
- modernization summary,
- inherited-vs-new design notes.

Do not award or remove dimension points merely because a remake is faithful or unfaithful. A future remake-comparison feature may evaluate that question separately.

# 18. Versioning rules

Rubric version is stored on every evaluation.

Breaking changes requiring new rubric version:
- dimension renamed with changed semantics,
- subcriteria added/removed,
- score scale changes,
- calculation changes.

Non-breaking editorial clarification:
- examples,
- wording,
- guidance that does not alter scoring behavior.

When rubric changes materially, do not silently compare old and new scores as though identical.

---

# 19. What we intentionally removed from Tomas Fit

Removed as public scoring mechanics:
- personal franchise dividend
- personal setting affinity
- personal archetype bonus
- personal social bonus
- personal horror oppression cap
- wrong-camera/form cap
- personal roguelike cap
- personal “buy status”
- Anchor research value
- weighted overall fit score
- personal score thresholds

Converted:
- franchise continuity → Story/Lore evidence when it meaningfully strengthens the game
- friction kill → Structure/Focus + experience tags
- horror oppression → Agency + tension/helplessness tags
- bloat → Pacing + Structure
- personal thematic resonance → Thematic/Emotional Impact
- execution trust → delivered Execution & Polish for released games

- Agency 7

Tags:

### Game B
- Story 5.5

- Agency 10
- Pacing 7
- Atmosphere 8

Tags:
combat-heavy, buildcraft, high punishment, repetition.

A conventional average can make them look similar.

# 21. Working dimension-score anchors

These anchors calibrate editorial use of the 0–10 dimension totals.

- **9.0–10.0 — Exceptional:** defining strength / reference-point quality.
- **8.0–8.5 — Strong:** clearly above ordinary execution and a meaningful reason to choose the game.
- **7.0–7.5 — Good / solid:** works well with visible limitations.
- **6.0–6.5 — Mixed / compromised:** functional but recurring limitations materially affect the experience.
- **5.0–5.5 — Weak:** some value remains, but shortcomings are persistent.
- **3.5–4.5 — Poor / limited:** contributes little or is repeatedly undermined.
- **0–3.0 — Absent / severely deficient:** reserved for genuinely absent offerings or extreme execution failure.

These are internal/editorial calibration terms, **not public school grades**.

A deliberately absent offering can legitimately score low without making the overall product bad. Example: conventional authored narrative in a survival sandbox.

## Active post-launch products

A released game may remain **Provisional** when substantial systems, balance, technical state or content continue changing quickly. Every profile retains an evidence/current-state cutoff.

---

# 22. Radar / spider visualization semantics

The rubric supports an eight-axis radar/spider visualization because all eight dimensions share the same 0–10 scale.

Rules:
- radar axis order may differ from database/display-list order but is globally fixed,
- unknown does not equal zero,
- no area-derived aggregate score,
- exact numeric rows remain available,
- comparison overlay should normally be limited to two games.

Recommended clockwise radar order:
1. Story & Character Investment
2. Thematic & Emotional Impact
3. Atmosphere & World Pull
4. Medium-Specific Craft
5. Agency & Satisfaction
6. Execution & Polish
7. Structure & Focus
8. Pacing & Time Respect
