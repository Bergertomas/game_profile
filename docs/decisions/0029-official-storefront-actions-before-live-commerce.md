# ADR 0029 — Official storefront actions before live commerce

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-08-26
**Context:** Master Plan v0.9 §11.4–11.5;
`Should_I_Play_Public_Product_Resolutions_2026-08-25.md` §10.

## Decision

Launch profiles provide a verified official completion point—“Where to play” or
equivalent—where platform and edition facts are reliable. Ordinary official
links are the baseline. Approved affiliate links may supplement or replace the
corresponding ordinary destination with clear disclosure, but a paying store
cannot hide a materially important non-paying store. Steam remains visible when
relevant even without an affiliate relationship.

The internal record distinguishes profile scope, edition, platform, region,
storefront, ordinary URL, affiliate URL, relationship, disclosure, source,
retrieval/verification time and staleness. A stale or unverifiable live offer is
removed conservatively; a still-verified official destination may remain.

Commerce cannot affect evaluation, coverage, Search/discovery ordering,
comparison summaries or which stores are materially disclosed. Compare is not a
price-comparison surface.

The rollout sequence is:

1. universal verified official action layer;
2. affiliate-ready data model and approved programs;
3. a limited live-commerce pilot on roughly 10–20 PC profiles;
4. evidence-based expansion into regional price, offer and subscription data.

Should I Play? does not process payments, sell keys, claim global price
coverage or include grey-market sellers under this contract.

## Consequences

- The quiet public release is not blocked by affiliate contracts or live price
  feeds.
- Destination provenance/freshness is data, not ad-hoc link copy.
- Commercial disclosure is public; commercial operations receive no editorial
  control.
