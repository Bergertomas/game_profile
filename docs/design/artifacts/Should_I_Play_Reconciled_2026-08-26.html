00 · mission & change ledger — reconciliation of a design lock

From fifteen to a hundred

This artifact reconciles “Should I Play? at Fifteen” — the public-MVP design lock — with the product contracts frozen since: Master Plan v0.9 and the public-product decision records of 24–25 August 2026. It is a surgical update, not a rediscovery: the brand, the eight-dimension rubric, the D3 profile, the Field Guide hybrid, the Compare model and the artwork rule all carry forward untouched. What changes is recorded in the ledger below, designed in §01–§09, and specified to implementation depth in §10–§15 — for the 12–15-profile private validation corpus and the ~100-profile quiet public release, desktop and 390 px both.

How to read this document

Real published shouldiplay.gg content (Alan Wake 2, Returnal, Redfall). Synthetic invented for this prototype — every value and sentence made up here, never editorial output. Proposed new behavior recommended for implementation. Later deliberately out of scope.

Locked marks a frozen product decision from the governing documents — not reopened here. Design marks a decision made in this pass — argued where it appears, reversible in review without touching a locked contract.

Frozen foundation — not reopened

  • Public brand Should I Play?; the Game Profile is the methodology. Exactly eight dimensions, 0–10, subcriteria-derived; no public or hidden aggregate; low is descriptive; Unknown, ranges, confidence and scope stay truthful.
  • D3 is the individual-profile foundation; the Field Guide hybrid governs the shell; Archivo + Newsreader, warm paper + graphite, hairline rules, one game-led accent that never means quality.
  • Compare is exactly two, subordinate to profiles, artwork-free, noindex at pair level; interval relations preserve range and Unknown; summary prominence is presentational, the rows are authoritative.
  • Mixed art/artless product — artless is a complete, designed state. Mobile is a first-class 390 px target. Static architecture: prerendered routes, no runtime database, no runtime LLM anywhere.
  • Refused, still: rankings, universal winners, match percentages, fake activity, badges, carousels, accounts, personalization (post-launch — and no disabled control or “coming soon” pretends otherwise).

What moved since the lock

  • Journey: Search (dominant) · Compare (major secondary) · What should I play? (progressive) replace the older Find / Compare / start-with-a-question framing — three capabilities, one hierarchy, never three equal tabs (§01).
  • Search grows a registry: four availability states, including recognized-but-unprofiled with a coverage request (§03).
  • Discovery ships: a bounded, deterministic “What should I play?” with visible, editable criteria — not a chatbot (§04).
  • Time becomes public data: Total commitment and Session suitability — two concepts, neither a ninth dimension (§05).
  • Profiles complete their purchase loop: Where to play, corrections-with-context, complimentary-access disclosure (§06); the homepage learns to be 15 and ~100 without changing grammar (§02).

01 · journey model Locked

Three paths, one hierarchy

The decision records replace Find / Compare / start-with-a-question with a ranked journey model. The header still names every real room — text only, no icons — but the header is not the hierarchy. The homepage is: one open field, one strong room, one italic question.

1 · dominant

Search

You already know the name — the overwhelmingly common case. An open field on the homepage, a /-focused trigger in every header. Two interactions from anywhere to a profile; four honest states when the catalog can't answer (§03).

2 · major secondary

Compare

Immediately available, never buried: a header room, the /compare launcher, a curated pair previewed on the homepage, and Compare ⇄ exits on every profile. Always exactly two profiles; always subordinate to them (§07).

3 · progressive

What should I play?

Needs-based discovery that discloses itself in steps: an italic question and a small preview on the homepage, a full room once engaged, straight to full results on a phone. Bounded and deterministic — curated entries, filters and plain language over the published catalog, never a chatbot (§04).

Why not three tabs · Three product capabilities flatten into three equal tabs only if the header is asked to carry the hierarchy. It isn't. The header names rooms (Search · Compare · What should I play? · How we score · About); the homepage ranks them by form — a field is not a link, and a link is not a question. §02 renders the ranking.

04 · what should I play? — bounded, deterministic, editable

Discovery that shows its reading

The third path is a small deterministic instrument, not a chatbot. Ordinary language is matched against the published vocabulary — dimensions, traits, session windows, commitment bands — and the reading is shown as criteria you can edit: strengthen, soften, or delete. Interpretation defaults are locked: ordinary positive language reads as Prefer, ordinary negative as Prefer not; only explicit hard words — must, only, require, no, nothing with — create a Must, except a concrete available-session statement, which is hard by default. Dimension thresholds come only from the governed labels: Strong or better (7.5+) and Exceptional (10). Results are grouped by evidence, each with its reasons; what the vocabulary cannot use is named, not silently dropped. The whole thing runs client-side over the build-emitted corpus: no runtime LLM, no service; query storage is governed per phase (§13).

What it refuses Locked

No match percentage — a criterion either holds, fails, or can't be verified. No hidden quality ordering — buckets are evidence classes; within a bucket, order is by criteria met, stated on the row. No open-ended AI promise — the field's affordance is a mood sentence, and the interpreter's whole vocabulary is published. No personalization — personal matching is post-launch, and no disabled control pretends otherwise. Query handling is stated truthfully per phase: the validation build is client-only with nothing stored; a raw-query research layer exists only under ADR 0028 — enabled, if ever, after notice, controls and a focused privacy review — and either way, no query is ever sent to a model.

Behavior — the strength control cycles Must include → Prefer → Prefer not → Must exclude; ✕ removes; every change recomputes live. Buckets are evidence classes: an explicit dimension threshold contradicted by an exact score excludes (counted, stated, never ranked); a score range that crosses the threshold, an Unknown, or Low confidence files under “Can't verify” — never a guess; “Near matches” exist only for governed relations — a hard session budget that falls inside the game's useful session window — with the reason named on the row; any other Must that can't be verified files under “Can't verify” with the reason; preferences produce meets and shortfalls on the row, and a failed preference always says what the game lacks, never that it “has” it. When nothing verifies, the surface offers to soften the Must that excluded most — one tap, visible in the ledger, reversible. The durable state is the URL: /play?c=… encodes the criteria ids, never the free text. On 390 px the homepage field opens this room directly and the criteria ledger starts collapsed to “Read as {{ critCount }} criteria — edit”; results lead.
Design · Criteria are rows in a ledger, not removable pills in a chip tray — the register grammar the product already owns. The strength word is the control; reading the ledger top to bottom reads the whole query. Interpretation is a synonym table over the published vocabulary (editorial data, admin-editable, same pattern as game.aliases) plus unit parsing for hours and minutes — the mapping ships with the vocabulary, so a misread is a data fix, not a model retrain.

05 · practical time — two concepts, not a ninth dimension Locked

Total commitment · session suitability

Time is now public, practical data on every profile — but it is descriptive metadata, like scope and platforms, never a scored dimension. Total commitment answers “how much of my life is this?”: a public band plus sourced focused / engaged / completionist figures, with engaged play governing the headline. Session suitability answers “does it fit my evenings?”: a useful session window, an interruption-flexibility reading, and one controlled summary sentence. They are different questions and never collapse into each other.

Total commitment — the contract

Bands (locked): Brief ≤10 h · Moderate >10–25 h · Substantial >25–50 h · Long >50–100 h · Extensive >100 h — always from the engaged figure. Three sourced estimates sit under the band: focused (main path, brisk), engaged (main path plus what it naturally pulls you into — the headline), completionist. Special kinds replace the band where a number would lie: Open-ended, Variable, Unknown, Not applicable — each with a sentence saying why. Every figure carries its sourcing line; no source, no figure.

Session suitability — the contract

Two governed fields. Useful session window (below it, a sitting achieves nothing): Very short 20–30 min · Short 30–60 · Longer 60–120 · Extended >2 h · Variable · Unknown · Not applicable. Interruption flexibility, inspectable, with one explanatory line: High · Medium · Low · Unknown · Not applicable. A controlled summary closes it, drawn from a fixed list (§13) that maps onto the two fields — never replacing them. When the axes disagree, the summary carries both; neither is rounded away. One axis can be unknown on its own. Editor-observed minute ranges live in the admin as evidence for the band, never as the public contract.

Why there is no overall score revised

Because the average of eight honest numbers is a dishonest ninth. A beautifully written, mechanically clumsy RPG and a storyless, mechanically perfect action game can land in the same place on any single scale — and they are completely different purchases. The profile keeps the eight apart so you can weigh the one that decides it for you.

Independence & funding revised

Self-funded. No advertising, no sponsored profiles, no affiliate links, no payment for coverage of any kind. Store links are ordinary official links; when a publisher provides a copy, the profile discloses it. Nothing here is paid for by anyone but the editor.

[Current truth, stated as fact. If affiliate programs ever arrive they are a separate, disclosed future state (§13 carries the proposed wording) — never a release dependency. → T2]

Complimentary access new

When a publisher provides a copy or code, the profile says so in its trust band — plainly, with the provider named and the words “weighed nowhere.” No conditions are accepted; access never buys coverage, timing, or a number.

Corrections revised

Found something wrong — a fact, a score's rationale, a broken source? Every profile and comparison carries a corrections link that mails corrections@shouldiplay.gg already labeled with the game, the scope, the build and the evidence cut-off, so you never have to describe which page you meant. Material corrections are versioned internally; nothing is silently rewritten.

What this will not become extended

No user accounts, no comment sections, no aggregate score, no rankings, no “top 10 games like…” content farming, no price-comparison tables, and no chatbot — the discovery page reads your words against a published vocabulary on your device, and that is all it does. Personal taste matching may come later; until it exists, nothing here pretends it does.

Carried forward, one string changed — Who writes it (now “the editor” — one person, deliberately; the single-judgment argument unchanged), How scoring works, What “Verified” means, Direct play. The public byline is locked to “the editor” everywhere (T8, resolved — not reopened); internal audit lineage (calibration rounds and the like) stays admin-side and never appears as public copy.

12 · accessible interaction & nonvisual equivalents

The words are the instrument

Every graphic on the product has a prose equivalent that carries the same claim — the radar is decoration over the rows, never the other way around. The contracts, per surface:

  • Radar (all sizes): aria-hidden; a sibling visually-hidden paragraph reads “Profile across 8 dimensions… Strongest… Lowest… Not scored for lack of evidence: …” — generated by the same function everywhere. Overlays name both games and end “The full score rows are the authoritative reading. No overall winner is computed.”
  • Instrument rows: real buttons with aria-expanded/aria-controls; each carries a hidden “Why this score? {dimension}: {value}{, range}.” Ranges read as “7.0 to 9.0, range”; Unknown reads as the words “Not scored”, never zero.
  • Search: role="combobox" + listbox + aria-activedescendant; ↑↓ ↵ esc as printed on the field; state changes announced via a polite live region (“6 matches”, “Recognized, not yet profiled”, “No match”). The request confirmation is the focus target after activation.
  • Criteria ledger: a list, not a tag cloud. The strength control is a button whose accessible name is “{criterion}: {strength}. Press to change”; changes announce the new strength. ✕ is “Remove {criterion}”. The conflict note is role="status". Result buckets are h2 headings, so a reader's outline is: read as → couldn't use → verified → near matches → trade-offs → can't verify. “Meets:” renders only when at least one verified meet exists — never an empty label.
  • Compare rows: each row's hidden text is “{dimension}: {A} {value}, {B} {value}. {relation}.” — the relation words are the nonvisual reading of the double scale. The sticky legend is aria-hidden (identity is restated in every row). Undetermined rows say so; nothing reads as zero.
  • Keyboard: / focuses search anywhere; focus order follows source order everywhere; the 2 px offset outline never suppressed; row expansion and strength cycling are plain Enter/Space activations — no custom key grammar beyond the search field's printed four.
  • Color & motion: accents come in measured lift/base tints, AA on their grounds, and never encode judgment — every accent-carried fact is also words. Confidence and status are words only, never shading. All hover/press motion strips under prefers-reduced-motion.

Acceptance criteria — verified before Phase-4 exit

  • Mobile search sheet: role="dialog" aria-modal="true"; focus moves into the field on open, is contained while open, and returns to the trigger on close (Esc and Close both).
  • Errors: every failure sentence (request failure, pair-404, index fetch) is programmatically associated with its control via aria-describedby or a role="status" region — never color or position alone.
  • Zoom & reflow: 200% zoom and 320 CSS-px reflow with no two-dimensional scrolling on any reading surface (the dual-tick scale may scroll with its row, never the page).
  • Contrast: verified per pairing in CI (bone on graphite, ink on paper, both accent tints on their grounds) — asserted at build, not assumed from the palette.
  • Targets & motion: every interactive target ≥44 px on touch layouts; all transitions stripped under reduced-motion; the sticky compare legend never traps scroll.
  • Nonvisual radar/row contracts: the §12 hidden-text contracts above are acceptance tests, not guidance — a missing sr paragraph fails the build.
